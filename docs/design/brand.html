FileCanopy brand and design system

This is the look of FileCanopy before we draw the screens: the logo, colors, fonts, icons and building blocks every page will share. The feel is warm and friendly, with leafy greens and soft rounded corners, and it comes in light and dark versions that follow your device. Try the theme switch on the left to see both.

01 · Logo

Three logo ideas

Each idea is shown large, at small sizes (the smallest is roughly a browser tab icon), and with the name beside it on light and dark backgrounds. You chose A, the canopy over a folder. It says "your files, sheltered" at a glance and stays readable even at the tiny tab size. Its leaf crown is now drawn symmetrically, so it sits straight on the folder.

02 · Color

A forest palette

One deep green, called Canopy, does the main work: buttons, links and the selected item. A bright Leaf green appears only in the logo and illustrations, because it's too light for text. The grays lean slightly green so everything feels like one family. Warning and error colors stay separate from the brand green, so a problem never looks like a normal button.

In dark mode the green gets lighter and softer instead of simply being flipped, so text stays easy to read. Every text color here passes the WCAG AA contrast level on its background (at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text).

03 · Type

Two fonts, each with one job

Bricolage Grotesque is used for headings. It's friendly and a little quirky, which gives FileCanopy its personality. Atkinson Hyperlegible is used for everything else. A braille institute designed it so letters that are easy to mix up, like I, l and 1, look clearly different. That matters for file names. Both fonts are free and open source, and the app will include them itself, so it never calls out to Google.

05 · Icons

Simple line icons

Icons are drawn with one even line weight and rounded ends, which matches the soft corners elsewhere. An icon always sits next to a word, or has a spoken label for screen readers, so nobody has to guess what it means. For the real app I suggest Lucide, a free icon set in the same style, adding only the icons we use.

07 · Voice

Plain and friendly words

FileCanopy talks like a helpful neighbor. It uses short sentences and everyday words, and it says what happened and what you can do about it. It never blames you, and it never shows a file name you aren't allowed to see.

08 · Accessibility

Built in from the start

The target is WCAG 2.2 level AA, the common standard for accessible websites. In plain terms, that means:

  • Text is readable against its background in both themes.
  • Everything works with a keyboard, and a clear blue ring shows where you are.
  • Screen readers announce buttons, dialogs, progress and undo messages.
  • Color never carries meaning alone. Badges and errors also have an icon and words.
  • Animations are turned off when your device asks for less motion.
  • Text can be zoomed to 200 percent and phone layouts never scroll sideways.
